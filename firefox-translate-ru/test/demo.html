Why do leaves change color in autumn?

Every autumn, forests across the northern hemisphere turn from green to gold, orange and red. The change looks sudden, but it is the result of a slow chemical process that begins weeks earlier, when the days grow shorter and the nights become cooler.

Chlorophyll and the hidden pigments

Leaves owe their green color to chlorophyll, the pigment that captures sunlight and turns it into chemical energy. Chlorophyll breaks down faster than it is replaced when light levels fall, and as it fades, other pigments that were there all along finally become visible. Carotenoids produce the yellows and oranges; anthocyanins, which the tree manufactures in autumn, are responsible for the deep reds.

“Autumn is a second spring when every leaf is a flower.” — Albert Camus
